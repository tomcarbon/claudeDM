Let us do a major refactor/update of the multiplayer logic today. In essence,
we're going to compartmentalize character and companions to each session, and
get the rules straight for joining, saving, resuming games, logging on/off,
persistence, and maybe some things I missed. We can do treeshaking, removal

As regards multiplayer, there's basically three types of users:

1) The host
2) The companion player(s)
3) The ghost, that is someone who is just watching the game (can be guest or a
   user who is logged in but not joined to the session).

Arguably, there's actually 5 types of users:
4) the 'guest' is the 4th user, but for purposes of observation, It can be
   grouped with the non-joined but logged on user.
5) There's an admin account, that has a few extra priviledges.

I'm most concerned about those first three basic types.

There are 2 basic modes of play: single player mode, and multiplayer mode.

Singleplayer mode is the host player, and NPC companions, with no Join options
for any of the companions.

Multiplayer is every other combination:

Mode of entry for single player: at the DM's green ready prompt (that is, not
when the DM is thinking), the player may submit their turn, they send BAU with
the button or the enter key, and the message is posted to the adventure box
with the player's instructions. This is pretty much BAU, and it's pretty
simple. Let's make sure the multiplayer functionality does not adversely
'infect' this single player mode.

Multiplayer. The 'Turn Mode' might be a good idea, but I don't think it's
working. We can probably start by removing this and its 3 options in the
settings.

Ok, here's some guidelines for the multiplayer mode.

A host may log out or disconnect at any time, or they may switch to a different
game in the same campaign or to a game in a different campaign. In all cases,
the state of the game is saved. The companion players suffer no loss of
continuity with their game; they may play their turns and their turns will be
saved as well.

After a host or a companion player plays their turn, it is displayed to the
adventure box, and saved to the session json (and character jsons if need be).
The player may retract their response to try again. What is new, populate the
original comment in the bar (the companion player might only want to make a
small correction).

For the host, companion player(s), and ghost, when they load a session, the
characters in the companion section (menu button) gets updated for all users
with the session's characters and those characters' characteristics. When a
companion player goes to the Companion menu item, they can see their character
highlighted in one color, and the other companion players (if any) highlighted
in another color (the host/ghost would see all the companion players in this
second color).

Here is the normal order of play. For sake of example, a game might have a
host, two players, and 3 NPCs. If a slot is not joined, the DM controls the
stated NPC (say, 'Drak'). Once a player joins, they will select their
character from their character pool as usual. That player/character will be
joined, across session changes, logoffs, etc, and preserved during gameplay.
Only by unjoining the session will the DM resume control of the original NPC
(Drak) in future turns.

The DM will not start until the host and all joined players have acted.
However, the host has the option to 'continue' without the input of 1 or more
companion players (regardless if these players are online or offline, or
watching/playing another session, etc). If the host decides to continue, the
DM will play for the character that that companion player had selected
(effectively playing for the player in their absense).

The companion player and the host need a high level system for knowing when
their turn in a given session is expecting their input (it's their turn to
play). Maybe the session widget could get a purple light for this.

One thing, the user might log in and log out right there at the Begin Adventure
screen. The session widgets need to be refreshed at that level as well, and
also when the host loads the session the indicator should turn green for
everyone.

Did I miss anything? Hopefully that all makes sense.

KEY ARCHITECTURAL DECISIONS:

1. SESSION-SCOPED CHARACTER DATA:
   Each session gets its own copy of character/NPC files in:
   data/players/<host-slug>/<campaignId>/sessions/<session-id>/characters/
   data/players/<host-slug>/<campaignId>/sessions/<session-id>/npcs/

   The DM engine reads/writes these session-scoped files, so changes in one
   session don't affect another.

2. SESSION-LEVEL DM ENGINE:
   sessionEngines: Map<sessionDbId, { engine, characterId, scenarioId, ... }>

   Any connected player can trigger the DM when all turns are submitted.
   Engine persists for 5 minutes after last disconnect (grace period).

3. UNIFIED TURN FLOW (replaces 3 turn modes):
   - Single player: Send fires DM immediately (no queueing)
   - Multiplayer: All players queue turns → auto-fire when all submitted →
     host can "Continue" to skip absent players

4. TURN PERSISTENCE:
   Pending turns saved to session JSON pendingTurns field, surviving
   disconnects and reconnects.

5. RETRACT WITH TEXT RESTORATION:
   When retracting a turn, the original text is sent back and populated
   into the input field.

6. SESSION WIDGET INDICATORS:
   - Purple dot: Your turn is expected
   - Green dot: Session active (last 5 min)
   - Red dot: No recent activity
